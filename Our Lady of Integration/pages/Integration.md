- Integration isn’t so much a practice as much as it is a description of the result of a way of being.
- The three points of the [[Integration triangle]] are representative of three states of being. The top, [[Enlightenment]], is a state of satisfaction (I love it). The lower two are states of dissatisfaction. The bottom left, [[philosophy]], is the state of dissatisfaction with a downward trajectory. Philosophy is a state of being that most closely approximates to awareness of the dissatisfaction (I don’t love it). The bottom right, [[practice]], is the state of dissatisfaction with an upward trajectory, approximating resolution to satiate or sooth the pain of dissatisfaction (I desire that which is loved).
- Integration, is the process that melds these three experiences together. Integration is the ether that exists between philosophy and practice, where a person creates [[meaning]] from [[suffering]] that produces a goal that can be accomplished; the ether between practice and enlightenment from which springs forth the [[faith]], [[hope]], and [[desire]] that something better can be realized; and when all three states of being are properly integrated, the individual experiences the state of enlightenment where they realize that “[[It’s all the same]].” Love, lack of love, and the desire for love; Philosophy, Practice, and Enlightenment; Satiation and dissatisfaction, they are all necessary colors or brushstrokes in a single work of art that is being a human.
- The concept of integration is not necessarily a logical one. Though the intent is to be logical about the importance of religion and spirituality by appealing to the anthropological and biological history and the ability to hack negative physiological phenomena via physical, emotional, spiritual, and therapeutic practice, integration is itself, an ineffable experience.
